5-Minute Brain Breaks

Five simple exercises you can do every day to keep your mind sharp — no equipment, no pen, just a few spare minutes wherever you are.

This is a free bonus PDF from Brain AI, made for you as a thank-you for taking the test.

1 Exercise One ~3 min

Count It Down

Pick any number between 40 and 100. Now count backward by 3s — 97, 94, 91 — for as long as you can, all in your head. No pen, no phone. If you lose your place, just pick a new number and start again.

Why it helps: Working with numbers this way keeps your working memory active — the same skill you use to hold a phone number or a grocery list in mind.

Visual idea: a descending staircase of numbers, one warm dot marking the starting point

2 Exercise Two ~2 min

Five Things I See

Look around wherever you're sitting or standing. Silently name five things you can see — a lamp, a sign, a cup, anything at all. Then close your eyes and try to recall all five, in the same order you named them.

Why it helps: This is a quick workout for short-term memory and attention — the two things people notice slipping first, and the two most worth practicing.

Visual idea: a simple open eye, looking out at five small everyday objects

3 Exercise Three ~2 min

Word Chain

Think of a category — fruits, countries, things in a kitchen, anything you like. Try to name five different words in that category before you count to twenty in your head. Pick a new category each time you play.

Why it helps: Finding the right word quickly is a skill of its own, separate from memory — this keeps that word-finding pathway warm.

Visual idea: three overlapping chain links, each holding a different short word

4 Exercise Four ~3 min

What Comes Next?

Make up a simple sequence of numbers, like 2, 5, 8, or 100, 90, 80. Figure out the pattern, then say the next two numbers out loud. Once it feels easy, try a harder jump — every 7s, or every 11s.

Why it helps: Spotting a pattern and predicting what's next is the same kind of thinking you use to plan a schedule or follow a recipe.

Visual idea: a rising row of dots with a dashed, question-mark dot at the end

5 Exercise Five ~2 min

Slow Breath, Sharp Mind

Breathe in slowly while you count to four. Hold for four. Breathe out for four. Repeat five times, counting each breath in your head. Let your shoulders drop a little more with every breath out.

Why it helps: A calm body gives your mind a clean, focused reset — and focus is what makes every other exercise here easier.

Visual idea: a soft, single-line breathing wave, calm and unhurried
